# DQIX Randomizer 1.5

This release gathers everything since 1.3 (1.4 was never released on its own).
The big change: **the randomizer is now an application**. Download
`DQIX-Randomizer.exe`, drop your ROM on the window, click **Randomize**.

## How to use it

1. You need your own copy of the **European multilingual** ROM:
   `Dragon Quest IX - Sentinels of the Starry Skies (Europe) (En,Fr,De,Es,It).nds`
   - Serial `YDQP`, MD5 `3a63438fff7db282fa3133e8fd020e85`, 268,435,456 bytes.
   - The application checks the file and refuses any other release. No ROM is
     distributed here.
2. Run `DQIX-Randomizer.exe`. There is nothing to install. The first time, Windows
   SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC": the program is simply not
   code-signed. Click *More info*, then *Run anyway*, or build it yourself
   from the source.
3. Drop your ROM on the window, pick a seed (or keep the random one), choose a
   preset and click **Randomize**. After about 25 seconds, a playable
   `DQIX-Randomizer-seed<seed>.nds` appears next to your ROM.
4. Start a **new game** in your emulator (melonDS, or DeSmuME with the Dynamic
   Recompiler turned off).

The ROM never leaves your computer: the application works offline and sends
nothing anywhere. The same seed with the same options always gives exactly the
same game, so a seed and the list of options are all you need to share a run.

The window is available in **English, French, German, Spanish and Italian**;
switch at any time from the menu at the top right.

## What's new since 1.3

- The application, with two presets (**Balanced** and **Total chaos**) and a
  checkbox for every rule.
- New randomizations: **spells**, **skill trees**, **monster drops at every
  battle**, **vocations**, **story bosses**.
- **Never the same monster twice on screen.**
- Balancing rules, each one available as an option: a share of consumables in
  containers, shop prices bounded by the story, red chests that keep their
  rarity.
- A fix for field monster models (see *Fixes*).

## Every rule, option by option

### Field monsters — *on in both presets*

- Each field monster is replaced with another one from the game's 256 field
  monsters. The overworld symbol, model, size, shadow and hitbox match the new
  monster.
- **Never the same species twice on screen.** When a monster appears, the
  randomizer draws again if that species is already walking around you. Stay in
  one field and you keep meeting new monsters.
- Only the 256 bestiary monsters roam: bosses never walk around the map.

### Chest and pot contents — *on in both presets*

Blue chests, pots, barrels, cupboards, red chests, and the item each monster
carries, can give any of the game's 1,090 regular items, weapons and armour
included. Every one of those items can be obtained somewhere.

| Rule | Balanced | Total chaos | Checkbox |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rank | rarer items come from rarer places: five-star legendary gear only from the highest-rank containers | legendary gear can turn up in the very first barrel | *rare items everywhere* |
| Consumables | the game has six times more equipment than consumables, so the randomizer gives consumables about a third of what containers hold | nine containers out of ten give equipment, as the raw draw does | *mostly equipment* |
| Red chests | a red chest keeps the star rating of the item it originally held, give or take one | a red chest can hold anything, even an ordinary item | *red chests without guarantee* |

### Monster drops at every battle — *on in both presets*

- Each time a monster drops an item, that item is drawn anew from the 1,088
  obtainable items: the same monster can give something different every time.
- Drop chances are those of the original game.
- **Balanced:** 4- and 5-star items stay rare. A drawn 4- or 5-star item is only
  kept once in eight draws, which works out at about one drop in 60.
- **Total chaos:** every item has the same chance, legendary gear included
  (*4- and 5-star items as common as the rest*).

### Shops — *on in both presets*

The 37 shops sell a new stock. It is drawn once, when the ROM is built, and
stays the same for the whole playthrough. 1,082 different items can appear, against
330 in the original game.

| Rule | Balanced | Total chaos | Checkbox |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prices | a shop never sells anything pricier than what the original game sells there; shops follow the story, so an early armoury stays affordable | the first village can stock endgame gear | *endgame gear for sale from the start* |
| Families | every slot keeps its family: an armourer sells armour, a grocery keeps its accessories; 4- and 5-star items are reserved for Dourbridge's secret shop and the two Stornway stalls that open after the final boss | any item in any shop; those three shops become ordinary ones | *any item in any shop* |

### Story bosses — *Total chaos only*

- Every scripted boss battle (story, lairs, fixed fights: 89 boss references)
  is fought against **another boss**, never an ordinary monster.
- A story boss keeps its stand-in until you beat it.
- The three Gittish generals bring a new boss for the rematch.
- The twelve **lair bosses** get a new one every time you enter their lair.
- **Never touched:** the prologue fight (two slimes and a cruelcumber against a
  level 1 hero with no party).
- **Hexagoon**, the first boss, stays himself by default: he is fought with no
  party, so any other boss in his place could make the run impossible. Untick
  *keep the first boss* to take that risk.

### Spells — *on in both presets*

- The spells a vocation learns as it levels up are redrawn from the game's 61
  spells.
- Levels and counts do not change: a Priest still learns seventeen spells, at
  the same seventeen levels.
- The three vocations without magic (Warrior, Martial Artist, Gladiator) stay
  without magic.
- **Balanced:** a vocation still learns weak spells early and strong ones late,
  following the game's own power scale.
- **Total chaos:** Omniheal can land at level 1 (*powerful spells from the
  start*).

### Skill trees — *on in both presets*

- All 286 skill-point milestones of the 26 trees are redealt: the 147
  abilities and the 139 stat bonuses.
- A milestone that gave an ability can now give a bonus, and the other way
  round.
- Menu and battle names always match what the milestone really gives.
- **Balanced:** milestones move **within** their tree, so a sword tree still
  grants sword techniques (a weapon technique needs that weapon in hand).
- **Total chaos:** milestones move across all 26 trees, by cost band (*mix the
  trees together*). A weapon bonus that moves keeps its weapon, and its name
  says which one ("Attack +30 with axe").
- The game keeps the milestones you learned inside your save: **start a new
  game** on each randomized ROM.

### Vocations — *on in both presets*

- **All twelve vocations** are offered at Alltrades Abbey from the start. The
  unlock quests still work.
- **Recruits:** a companion created at Patty's gets one of the twelve
  vocations at random; her menu only offers "Random".
- **The hero** starts a new game with a vocation drawn from the seed instead of
  Minstrel. During the prologue he is still shown as a Guardian, as in the
  original game; the drawn vocation shows after the fall.
- **Locked vocations** (optional challenge, off in both presets): the abbot
  refuses to change anyone's vocation, with a message in all five languages.
  The "Voice of Vocation" is blocked in the code too (not yet seen in
  play). Revocation (back to level 1 in the same
  vocation) stays allowed. Quests that need you to practise another vocation
  can no longer be done.

### Monster stats — *experimental, off in both presets*

HP, attack, defence, agility, experience and gold are swapped between all
monsters, bosses included, with no bound: an early slime can get a late boss's
HP. The game reads the new values correctly, but nobody has played a full run
with this option yet.

## Rules that always apply

- **Key items** are never moved. The Magic Key and the Ultimate Key stay where
  they are.
- The **chronocrystal shop** is never touched.
- The **prologue battle** is never changed.
- Only the European multilingual ROM is accepted; anything else is refused
  rather than turned into a broken game.
- Balancing is always an option, never forced: every balanced rule has its own
  checkbox.

## Fixes

- **Field monster models.** Since 1.2.1, loading a monster's model on demand
  could fill the model memory with copies of the same monster. You saw few
  different monsters around, monsters wearing another monster's model, and
  sometimes a freeze after walking in the same field. Each monster that
  appears now loads its own model, and a model still shown on screen is never
  unloaded.
- **Skill-tree bonuses gave the wrong effect.** A moved bonus used to keep the
  type of the milestone it landed on: "Attack +30 with" on a former Max HP
  milestone gave +30 max HP. The whole milestone now moves.
- Skill-tree menus showed the original names after abilities had moved.
- A blank line could appear in a skill tree, and "Attack +30 with" could lose
  its weapon icon.
- A red chest could hold any item regardless of the dungeon.

## Known issues

- When many monsters are on screen and the model memory is full, a monster can
  still appear with another monster's model until one of them leaves.
- Sometimes a battle's command window turns light blue with dark text, or a
  monster shows the wrong colours. The cause has not been found yet.
- Treasure-map grottos and quest rewards are not randomized.
- Emulator only; not tested on real hardware or flashcarts. Savestates only
  work with the exact ROM they were made on.
- Windows may flag the unsigned `.exe` (SmartScreen, some antivirus programs):
  a known false positive with PyInstaller programs. Building from source
  avoids it.

## Building it yourself

```bash
git checkout v1.5
pip install ndspy keystone-engine capstone pyinstaller tkinterdnd2
python interface.py                 # the window, from source
python scripts/build_exe.py         # or build dist/DQIX-Randomizer.exe
```

Command line, same result as the window's presets (the seed is an example):

```bash
# Balanced
python randomizer.py "<rom>.nds" --seed 1 --sans-rotation --place --blob --plafond 1 --garde 163840 --sorts --aptitudes --butin-combat --vocations --vocations-embauche --vocation-depart -o out.nds
# Total chaos
python randomizer.py "<rom>.nds" --seed 1 --sans-rotation --place --blob --plafond 1 --garde 163840 --chaos-total -o out.nds
```
